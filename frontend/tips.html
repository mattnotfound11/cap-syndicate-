How-to

How to measure your head size for a cap

Snapbacks and dad caps adjust, so most heads are covered. Fitted caps don't. If you're buying a fitted, take two minutes and measure first.

What you need

  • A soft measuring tape (the sewing kind). No tape? Use a string or a charger cable and a ruler.
  • A mirror, or a friend.

How to do it

  1. Wrap the tape around your head about one finger above your eyebrows and just above your ears.
  2. Go around the back, over the widest part of your head. That's where the cap sits.
  3. Keep it snug but not tight. You should be able to slide one finger under it.
  4. Read the number in centimeters. Do it twice to be sure.
  5. Find your number in the size guide below.

Between two sizes? Go one size up for fitted caps. A slightly loose fitted is fine. A tight one gives you a headache by lunch.

Care

How to clean a cap without ruining the shape

Sweat, dust and that one time it rained on the jeep. Caps get dirty. Here's how to clean one and keep the shape.

Never do this

  • No washing machine and no dryer. The tumbling bends the brim and crushes the crown.
  • No bleach and no hot water. They fade the color and can shrink wool.

Do this instead

  1. Test first. Dab a little soapy water on the inside. If color comes off, spot clean only.
  2. Mix a gentle wash. Cold water plus a few drops of mild detergent or dish soap.
  3. Scrub light. Use a soft toothbrush in small circles. Start with the sweatband inside. That's where most of the dirt is.
  4. Rinse by dabbing. Wipe the soap off with a clean damp cloth. Don't soak the whole cap.
  5. Dry it in shape. Stuff the crown with a small towel or put it over a bowl. Let it air dry in the shade. Direct sun fades color fast.

Style

5 ways to style a cap with different outfits

  1. Everyday heat. Plain tee, shorts, sneakers and a snapback. Match the cap color to your shoes and the fit looks planned.
  2. Aircon mall, night out. Oversized hoodie, cargo pants and a fitted. A dark fitted with a hoodie is the easiest clean look there is.
  3. Smart casual. Polo or button-up, slacks or chinos, and a dad cap in a neutral color. Low profile, so it doesn't fight the outfit.
  4. All black, one pop. Black top, black bottoms, then a red cap. One loud piece on a quiet outfit always works.
  5. Rainy season or the beach. Bucket hat, tank or jersey, shorts and slides. Bucket hats keep the rain and the sun off your face.

Quick rules: keep the brim clean and straight if it's flat, and pull the cap down to sit level, not perched on top. Backwards is fine. Just commit to it.

Guide

Snapback vs fitted vs dad cap: which one is for you

Snapback

Flat brim, stiff front, plastic snap in the back. One size fits most. Bold and very street. Pick this if you want the cap to be the loudest part of the fit.

Fitted

Closed back, sold in exact sizes. The cleanest shape of the three because there's no strap or gap. Pick this if you know your size (or just measured it) and want the sharpest look.

Dad cap

Soft, unstructured crown, curved brim and an adjustable strap. Low profile and comfortable all day. Pick this if you want something easy that goes with anything.

Still deciding?

  • First cap ever: dad cap.
  • Want to stand out: snapback.
  • Want it to look made for your head: fitted.
  • Hot or rainy day: try a bucket hat.

Trivia

Cap trivia: where the baseball cap came from

  • It started on the field. The Brooklyn Excelsiors baseball team is often credited with wearing an early version of the cap in 1860: a rounded crown with a short brim.
  • The brim grew for a reason. Players needed to block the sun, so brims got longer and stiffer over the years.
  • The fitted shape is from 1954. That's when New Era released its 59FIFTY, the fitted style pro baseball still wears today.
  • Snapbacks hit the streets in the '80s and '90s. Hip-hop artists and team merch made them a style piece, not just sports gear. They came back hard in the 2010s.
  • Backwards has a history too. Catchers flipped their caps backwards so their masks would fit.
  • Fitted sizes are math. The number is roughly your head size in inches divided by pi (3.14). A 22-inch head works out to about size 7.
